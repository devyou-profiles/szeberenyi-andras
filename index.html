No. 1

Karasu, Türkiye

Sep 2021

Young people in healthy life hand in hand

What was the project about?

My very first Erasmus+ youth exchange took me to Karasu, on Türkiye’s Black Sea coast, in September 2021. As the title “Young people in healthy life hand in hand” suggests, the project was about healthy living – and about how much easier it is to build good habits together.

Through non-formal education activities we talked about nutrition, regular exercise and mental well-being, shared good practices from our own countries and looked at how young people can motivate each other to live more actively.

Sport was at the heart of the week: we played traditional games in the Karasu sports hall, worked in international teams and spent time together on the Black Sea shore. It was the experience that started my Erasmus+ journey – and showed me how quickly strangers become friends.

No. 2

Pervolia, Cyprus

Dec 2021Group Leader

EUROGAMES

What was the project about?

The project brought together young people from Cyprus, Romania, Greece, France, Hungary, Poland, Italy, and Spain interested in:

  • Exploring diversity through sports and other intercultural activities, teaching each other little cultural secrets and highlighting their similarities;
  • Discussing current issues related to general well-being, nutrition, and sports by exchanging good practices and experiences regarding the prevention of diseases caused by a lack of physical exercise and unhealthy eating habits;
  • Promoting a healthy lifestyle and sports as a tool for social inclusion to combat marginalization, xenophobia, and stereotypes (at local, national, and European levels).
No. 3

Agros, Cyprus

Jul 2022

Cultural heritage as a path in migrants inclusion

What was the project about?

The project, which was attended by 40 young people from Cyprus, Greece, Hungary and Romania, focused on the inclusion of refugees through cultural heritage.

The diverse cultural environment of Cyprus and other countries gives a positive image of the peaceful coexistence of people with different cultural identities. Cultural and religious heritage, associated with important stages of human history, has universal value and is part of the common heritage of humanity. The use of cultural heritage as an educational tool can help to address social issues, to bring people living in Europe closer together and to promote multicultural dialogue.

The daily programme included non-formal education activities and workshops: team-building games, videos, simulation games, hands-on activities, exchange of good practices, cultural educational visits, reflection groups, intercultural evenings, creative actions/activities, sports and physical activities.

No. 4

Arrouquelas, Portugal

Sep 2022

Climate Change? NO! Change Yourself!

What was the project about?

Young people aged 18–30 from ten countries – Hungary, Bulgaria, Portugal, Malta, Spain, Croatia, Estonia, Italy, the Czech Republic and Poland – came together in Arrouquelas to talk about climate change and what each of us can do about it.

In workshops we explored the main climate problems and possible solutions, made and presented posters, and met local organisations who spoke about forest fires and drought. A game showing how the world’s population and GDP are divided, and a test of our own environmental footprint, showed us that the problems are bigger than we thought.

There was plenty of adventure too: a trip to the mountains and a lookout tower, wild-water canoeing on the crystal-clear Zêzere river (my favourite moment, apart from the ocean), a team city game, grape harvesting and treading wine the traditional way, intercultural evenings with traditional food, and a flash mob we performed in town. On the last day we explored Lisbon before flying home.

No. 5

Madrid, Spain

Sep 2023

Defending the rights of minorities

What was the project about?

In September 2023 we took part in an Erasmus+ youth exchange in Madrid about the situation of minorities in different countries. Young people from Bulgaria, Hungary, Latvia, Türkiye, North Macedonia, Poland and Spain took part.

We talked about the problems minorities and refugees face in our countries, compared our experiences, and learned how Spanish people themselves see their own country.

We spent a wonderful time together, and everyone went home with great experiences and a lot of new knowledge. If you haven’t taken part in a project yet, you definitely should – it’s an experience you won’t forget!

No. 6

Elafonisos, Greece

Sep 2026

Y-Cycle

What was the project about?

A cycle of ideas, skills and positive change. Y-Cycle was an Erasmus+ youth exchange created by Community Cohorts in Greece, together with partner organisations from Portugal, Hungary and Romania.

The project brought young people together to explore employment, entrepreneurship, social innovation, digital tools and the circular economy.

Through non-formal education, international teamwork and practical challenges, we discovered how our ideas can become realistic initiatives that create social, environmental and economic value.
